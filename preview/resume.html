Prompt engineering reflection

I ran three prompts. The first set the terms: "You are a recruiter screening candidates for a product and growth internship in consumer tech. Do not rewrite my resume. List the specific changes you would make to wording, skills, experience descriptions, organization and emphasis, and explain why each matters for this role." The second narrowed it: "For each bullet, say whether it describes a task or an outcome, and what measurable result is missing." The third pressure-tested it: "Which three lines would make you stop reading, and what is the strongest thing here that I have buried?"

The changes were structural, not factual. Product work moved above education. My four service jobs became a single "Customer-Facing & Operations" block, which freed most of a page without dropping anything true. Task bullets became outcome bullets: "built an AI concierge" became "shipped a seven-agent AI concierge for under $1,000 that plans a full night out in six seconds."

The real lesson was about the prompt. "Make my resume better" produced flattery. Assigning a role, forbidding a rewrite and demanding reasons produced a critique I could act on.
