# Blog comments + your replies (rubric: 8 pts for comments, 8 pts for responses)

## How to use this file

The comments must come from **real people** — friends, family, classmates,
teammates. You cannot post them yourself from four fake accounts; the professor
can see that every comment came from one IP and one browser.

**The workflow that actually works:**

1. Publish the blog post.
2. Text four people the direct link to the post.
3. Send each person a *different* one of the prompts below — a nudge about what
   to react to, not a script to copy. Their own words are the point.
4. As they come in: **Dashboard → Comments → Approve**.
5. Reply to each one using **Reply** on the comment row (this is what makes it a
   threaded response and shows the "Author" badge on your site).

> Your replies must be posted while you are **logged in as yourself**, otherwise
> WordPress will not mark them as author replies and the threading looks wrong.

---

## Nudges to send (one per person)

**Person 1 — someone who has been scammed or almost scammed**
> "Wrote a post about fake restaurant reviews for my IS class. Have you ever
> gotten burned by a review or an ad that turned out to be fake? Would love a
> comment on it — link's here."

**Person 2 — someone in the restaurant / service industry**
> "You've worked in restaurants — does the review thing in this post match what
> you actually saw from the inside? Comment on it if you get a sec."

**Person 3 — a classmate or fellow student**
> "This is my blog post for IS101. Curious whether the five checks at the end
> are things you'd actually do, or whether that's too much work in real life.
> Drop a comment."

**Person 4 — a parent / family member / someone older**
> "Wrote about how fake reviews work. Wondering if this is something you'd have
> caught, or if the whole thing sounds new. Leave me a comment on it."

## Your four replies

Write these yourself in the moment — but here is the shape of a reply that earns
full credit. Each one should **name something specific the commenter said** and
**add something the post did not already say.** A reply that just says "thanks
for reading!" four times reads as filler.

### Reply pattern

1. Acknowledge their specific point (not a generic thank-you).
2. Add one new fact, example, or nuance.
3. Ask them something back, or close with a concrete takeaway.

### Worked examples — adapt these to whatever they actually write

**If someone shares their own scam story:**
> That's the part that gets people — it wasn't a badly-spelled email, it looked
> completely normal. The pattern you're describing is why I stopped trusting the
> headline number entirely. The tell in your case was probably the urgency;
> almost every one of these works by removing your time to think. Did you end up
> reporting it anywhere, or just eat the loss?

**If someone from the industry confirms it:**
> Hearing it from inside the business is the part I couldn't get from the data.
> I could see the bursts in the timestamps, but I had no idea the "leave us a
> five-star review for a free app" cards were that routine. That's the piece I'd
> add to the post if I rewrote it — the manipulation isn't always a review farm
> overseas, sometimes it's just a printed card at the register.

**If a classmate says the five checks are too much work:**
> Fair, and honestly I don't run all five either. If I only had ten seconds I'd
> do exactly one: sort by newest and look at the dates. Bursts of reviews with
> dead air between them catch most of it, and it takes about three seconds. The
> other four are for when there's real money on the line.

**If a family member says this is all new to them:**
> That's exactly why it works so well — it doesn't feel like a scam, it feels
> like a list. The thing I'd tell anyone who isn't looking for it: the number of
> reviews is doing more persuading than the reviews themselves. Next time
> something has a suspiciously perfect score, read the three-star ones first.
> They're the only reviews nobody has a reason to fake.

- [ ] 4 comments visible on the published post (not pending)
- [ ] All 4 comments are from different people / different email addresses
- [ ] 4 replies posted, each threaded **under** its comment
